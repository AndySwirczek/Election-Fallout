*Prepared Nov. 4, 2026 for an MHA briefing. Research cutoff: web sources available Nov. 3–4, 2026.*

**Political frame [I]:** Under the user-provided results (Gov.-elect Benson, Dem House and Senate, AG-elect Savit), the GOP House under Speaker Matt Hall loses control in January 2027. ## 1. Federal H.R. 1 (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21): Medicaid impacts on Michigan

**Status [V]:**
- **Enrollment base:** In March 2026, "nearly 1.7 million" Michiganders were on Medicaid, "over 526,000" of them in the Healthy Michigan Plan (HMP). *(CHRT Medicaid Policy Brief, May 22, 2026.)* Bridge Michigan has cited "nearly 650,000" on HMP [S]. The figures probably differ by date or definition.
- **Work requirements:** These must be implemented by **Jan. 1, 2027** for the expansion (HMP) population. The requirement is **80 hours/month** of community engagement, and outreach to enrollees was required to begin by Sept. 30, 2026 *(CHRT, May 2026)*. MDHHS says the rules apply to current enrollees at renewals due **on or after March 1, 2027** [S: MDHHS HMP pages/newsroom, July 23, 2026].
- **Coverage-loss estimates (they vary):**
  - MDHHS testimony to the House subcommittee (Apr. 28, 2026): about **80,000 at risk in month 1** (the March 2027 cohort) and about **100,000 expected to lose coverage in year 1** [S, PDF not directly readable].
  - FY2027 Executive Budget materials, as cited by third parties: about **200,000** [S].
  - Advocacy coalition: up to **512,000** [S].
- **Six-month renewals** for HMP. Retroactive coverage falls from 3 months to 1 month for expansion enrollees and to 2 months for traditional Medicaid *(CHRT)* [V].
- **Provider taxes:** Michigan's hospital and nursing facility taxes run **5.01%–5.5%**. Provider taxes fund about **20% (~$3B)** of the state share of Medicaid. Starting 2028 (federal FY), rates must step down **0.5 pt/year to 3.5% by 2032**. Nursing facility taxes are exempt, so the cut falls on hospitals. The projected State Medicaid Fund loss is **>$1B in 2028** (RAND, cited by CHRT). Each $1B of lost state revenue means about $1.88B in lost federal match, **$2.88B total** *(CHRT)* [V].
- **Insurance Provider Assessment (IPA):** HR1 and a **Feb. 2, 2026 CMS final rule** make Michigan's Medicaid-plan-only IPA impermissible, with a transition period. CHRT puts the IPA at "nearly $500 million annually" to the state budget [V]. Bridge Michigan (Oct. 2025) reported about $650M/year collected plus $1.75B federal match per the SFA, and that a 2025 state law lets MDHHS create a replacement insurer tax [S/V via fetch summary]. These figures differ by source and year.
- **State directed payments (SDPs):** New SDPs are capped at Medicare rates, and existing SDPs above Medicare phase down starting 2028. Affected Michigan SDPs are the **Hospital Rate Adjustment (HRA) at $5.07B** and the **Specialty Network Access Fee (SNAF) at $610M** (MDHHS SNAPMA presentation, cited by CHRT). One model (Commonwealth Fund, cited by CHRT) estimates a **45% reduction in Medicaid payments to Michigan hospitals** [V]. A claimed "27% managed-care payment reduction" from a July 2026 CHRT brief could **not** be verified, so it is omitted.
- **CHRT's estimated reductions to the state Medicaid fund:** work requirements about **$615M**, provider taxes about **$1B**, six-month redeterminations about **$63M** [V].
- **Hospital-level dollar loss:** MHA told House Government Operations (June 25, 2026) that existing Medicaid reimbursement cuts and enrollment reductions are "already projected to cut hospital funding by **more than $6.5 billion over six years**." It also said nearly a quarter of Michigan hospitals operate at a loss [V: MHA newsroom]. A 2025 MDHHS figure of **$15B over 10 years** to hospitals and doctors and **$2.5B/yr** once fully implemented (Detroit News, July 10, 2025) is [S], because the article is paywalled.
- **Rural Health Transformation Program:** Michigan's FY2026 award is **$173,128,201**, announced Dec. 29–30, 2025 [S: MDHHS]. Per Bridge (Sept. 28, 2026, updated Oct. 19) [V]: $124.6M was allocated to 236 subrecipients and $48.4M remained to be allocated as of Sept. 22. Funds had to be obligated by Oct. 30, 2026 and spent by Sept. 2027. MHA itself received **$8.6M**. Rural hospital leaders say "very few of those dollars are actually going to hospitals." Michigan got less than Ohio ($202M) and Indiana ($206M), and the award was below the state's request [S: HFA].

**Why it matters for hospitals [I]:** HRA is the single largest hospital Medicaid lever. The QAF/HRA mechanism plus the SDP caps make up most of the multi-year exposure, and the coverage losses from 2027 onward add uncompensated care on top.

**Likely direction under a Dem trifecta [I]:** Expect maximal federal flexibility-seeking on exemptions, ex parte verification, and navigator funding. Expect state backfill debates (CRC has estimated ~$680M of state GF needed by FY2031–32 to cover the provider-tax decline [S via Bridge]), and AG Savit litigation against federal implementation actions viewed as unlawful. Note that a trifecta cannot undo the federal caps, so the fight shifts to replacement revenue and GF backfill.

## 2. Healthy Michigan Plan statutory "trigger"

**Status [V], with a correction to the premise:** The original 2013 law (MCL 400.105d) ended HMP if annual state savings fell short of program costs. Per HFA, that **cost/savings trigger was repealed in 2023** by **HB 4495, Public Act 98 of 2023** (signed July 18, 2023), which amended MCL 400.105d/400.106 and repealed 400.105c and 105f. I fetched the current MCL 400.105d and it contains no "savings," "cease," or federal-match-percentage termination language. MDHHS Executive Directive 2025-3 reportedly states Michigan is **not** among the 12 expansion states with trigger laws [S, PDF 403]. Michigan Advance (July 19, 2023) reported that the law "removes a requirement that triggers the elimination of Healthy Michigan if the program's net costs outweigh the net savings" [S, 403].

**Residual risk [I]:** There is no automatic sunset, but HMP still depends on annual appropriations. The FY27 House budget kept hospital provider-tax revenue in "boilerplate contingency" [S: HFA summary 4/16/2026], and the FY26 budget moved about $6B of hospital-tax and match authority into contingency [S via Bridge]. A future federal FMAP cut for expansion would also be a pure budget fight.

**Direction [I]:** A Dem trifecta is very unlikely to reinstate any trigger. Expect HMP to be defended through appropriations.

## 3. State budget: FY2026 and FY2027; QAF/HRA, Medicaid rates, GME

**FY2026 [V]:** The budget was late (signed **Oct. 7, 2025**, HB 4706 / PA 22 of 2025) after the House (Hall) vs. Senate (Brinks) standoff. MHA said it provided full Medicaid/HMP funding, full recognition of hospital provider taxes "with access to those funds without additional legislative action," restored SNAF, continued rural and OB stabilization pools, preserved Maternal Levels of Care verification, and restored MC3. MHA had earlier warned the House plan contained >$2.5B in hospital cuts [S].

**FY2027 [V/S]:** Leaders announced a deal on **July 3, 2026**, and Whitmer signed it **July 21, 2026**, on time this year.
- Announced size: **$75.2B** total, $14.1B GF, $19.8B school aid [S]. Citizens Research Council says the true total is about **$86.7B** because some federal and Medicaid dollars were moved off-budget [S]. Hall: "the most decisive victory for the republicans in a budget in a long time" [S].
- Earlier versions [V: MHA 4/24 and 5/1/2026]:
  - House HB 5619 carried a **$300M unspecified Medicaid savings** cut.
  - Senate SB 857/878 had no unspecified savings and used caseload, Most Favored Nation drug pricing, and efficiency savings instead.
  - Both recognized hospital provider taxes, SNAF, rural and OB pools, and Maternal Levels of Care.
  - House added $22M plus staff for H.R.1 work reporting.
- Final: the governor's release lists **$185M** "to stabilize Medicaid funding through efficiency savings identified with stakeholders" [S]. Other items [S]: $28M for Michigan Works (work-requirement referrals), a $20M supplemental for community health navigators, $13.5M in targeted Medicaid rate enhancements (nursing home capital, portable x-ray, private duty nursing), and $31M in behavioral health capacity tied to a new state psychiatric hospital.
- **MHA (July 3, 2026) [V]:** Brian Peters thanked lawmakers for supporting HMP, Medicaid health plans, obstetrical services, and rural and critical access hospitals. The statement gave no dollar figures.
- **GME, and HRA/QAF line amounts in the final FY27 budget:** **Not verified.** I found no source giving FY27 GME or HRA/QAF dollar amounts. Check the SFA/HFA enacted-budget summaries.

**Direction [I]:** FY2028 (Benson's first budget, early 2027) will be the first budget to absorb the provider-tax step-down (federal FY2028) and the IPA transition. Expect pressure to backfill with GF, a restructured IPA replacement, and continued hospital-tax recognition. GME is likely protected but competing with backfill needs.

## 4. House GOP hospital cost review board / consolidation package (HBs 6116–6119)

This is not in the original issue list, but it is the biggest live state threat MHA faced in 2026.

**Status [V]:** Introduced **June 18, 2026** by Speaker Hall (with Reps. Harris and DeBoyer). Tie-barred, and referred to House Government Operations. The testimony was June 25. **No action after June 23** per the legislature site. HFA analysis is dated 8/31/2026.

Provisions per MHA (June 22, 2026) [V]:
- 5-member Hospital Cost Review Board
- **12% tax on M&A purchase price**
- mergers allowed only with ≥2% rate reductions
- reimbursement capped at **200% of Medicare**, cash prices at **150%**
- a 10% price cut or loss of nonprofit status
- price increases capped at inflation
- market-share caps (8% statewide beds, 3% statewide market, 15% regional)

HB 6119 restricts physician noncompetes [S].

MHA's estimates [V]: the package would remove an **additional $2.3B/yr**, double hospitals in the red to **54**, and eliminate **21,600 jobs (9,000 RNs)**. A business coalition also opposed it [S]. Hall has tied medical-debt legislation to "hospital price transparency and mechanisms to rein in out of control hospital costs" [S].

**Direction [I]:** The package dies with the GOP House majority unless it moves in lame duck, which looks unlikely given no committee vote. But the **themes survive under Democrats in a different form:**
- Benson's platform: upfront hospital price disclosure, an online reporting portal, and AG enforcement
- AG-elect Savit's pledge to scrutinize hospital mergers
- a Senate-side "Healthcare Consolidation Prevention Act," referenced by MHA as pending with no action [V: MHA July 10, 2026; bill details not found]

## 5. Nurse staffing ratios (Safe Patient Care Act)

**Status [S]:** No 2025–26 session bill numbers or committee action were found. The prior-session package (SB 334–336; HB 4550–4552, 2023–24) would have set ratios such as 1:1 in critical care and 1:4 in med-surg, limited mandatory overtime, and required public posting of staffing. It never reached a floor vote and died. MHA previously estimated **5,100 beds** would have to close if ratios were enacted at current staffing [S, older estimate]. The **Michigan Nurses Association endorsed Benson** (Feb. 2026) [S].

**Why it matters [I]:** Ratio mandates are a top cost and capacity risk for hospitals amid H.R.1 revenue cuts.

**Direction [I]:** This is the highest-probability *new* labor-backed mandate under a trifecta, with MNA and AFL-CIO support and a Benson endorsement. Expect reintroduction in 2027. Bipartisan sponsorship in past sessions helps it. MHA's fiscal-impact case, made more urgent by H.R.1, will be its main counter. A compromise such as staffing committees, disclosure, or overtime limits without hard ratios is plausible.

## 7. 340B contract pharmacy protection

**Status [V]:** **SB 94** (Sen. Sam Singh, D) bars manufacturer restrictions on 340B contract pharmacy delivery and is tie-barred with **SB 95** (Sen. Lindsey, R; hospital price-transparency compliance). It is MHA-driven. It **passed the Senate 33-3 on March 6, 2025** and was referred to House Health Policy, where there has been **no recorded House action**. A House 340B bill had a hearing Sept. 24, 2025 [S: 340B Report]. The 2023–24 predecessors also passed the Senate and died in the House. PhRMA opposes it.

**Direction [I]:** It is likely to pass early in 2027 under a Dem House if it does not move in lame duck. Expect the SB 95-style transparency and reporting tie-bar to remain the price of passage. Federal 340B reform (e.g., Sen. Cassidy's framework, Aug. 2026 comments) could interact with it.

## 9. PBM laws

**Status [S]:** Michigan enacted PBM licensure, disclosure, and a Medicaid spread-pricing ban in **2022**, plus HB 4276 (2023, fee disclosure to MDHHS). The licensure act took effect Jan. 1, 2024. **HB 5303** (introduced Dec. 2, 2025) on Medicaid managed-care PBM contracts has no verified action. Federal PBM reforms were enacted in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (signed Feb. 3, 2026), including 100% rebate pass-through requirements [S: Milliman].

**Direction [I]:** Benson proposes a **prescription drug affordability board** [V]. A PDAB is likely in 2027–28, and PBM tightening is likely. Hospitals should watch how PDAB upper payment limits interact with 340B margins.

## 10. Medical debt

**Status [V]:** Two parallel packages passed opposite chambers, and **none are enacted** as of the latest records found.
- **Senate:** **SBs 449–451, 701–702** (Sens. Sarah Anthony, D, and Jonathan Lindsey, R) passed **March 11, 2026**. They would:
  - codify hospital financial-assistance policies (Hospital Financial Assistance Act)
  - add FAP reporting
  - keep medical debt off credit reports (Medical Debt Act)
  - cap interest and late fees at 3%/yr after a 90-day grace period
  - largely ban liens, garnishment, and foreclosure for medical debt [S on the details]
  MHA "secured several key amendments."
- **House:** **HBs 5254–5255, 6071–6073** passed the week of June 22, 2026. HB 6073 passed **94-12** on June 23. In the Senate they were referred to Committee of the Whole on June 25, with no later action. MHA: "The MHA has not taken a position on this legislation."
- Separately, the state has erased about **$200M** in medical debt (governor's office) [S].

**Direction [I]:** It is likely enacted in lame duck or early 2027. Under Democrats, expect the Senate (Anthony) version as the base. Benson also campaigned on partnering with hospitals to limit "aggressive" collections. Hall's demand to attach hospital price-transparency and cost-control provisions loses leverage after January.

## 11. ACA enhanced subsidy expiration and the Michigan marketplace

**Status [V]:** The enhanced premium tax credits **expired Dec. 31, 2025**. Per Bridge (July 13, 2026):
- effectuated marketplace enrollment was about **357,000 in Feb. 2026**, down about **130,000 (27%)** from Feb. 2025
- the record high was **501,806 (June 2025)**
- 2026 individual-market premiums rose about **20%** (DIFS-approved average 20.2% [S])

A U.S. House-passed 3-year extension was reported, but **enactment was not verified** [S].

**Why it matters [I]:** More uninsured and underinsured patients, on top of Medicaid losses, increase bad debt and charity care. This is particularly acute for rural hospitals.

**Direction [I]:** States can't replace federal PTCs cheaply. Options include a state subsidy program, a state-based exchange (Michigan uses HealthCare.gov), or reinsurance. A Dem trifecta may study a state-based marketplace or targeted subsidies, constrained by H.R.1 budget pressure.

**Sources:** https://bridgemi.com/michigan-health-watch/michigan-affordable-care-act-marketplace-enrollment-slumps-amid-rising-costs/ · https://www.michiganpublic.org/health/2026-01-20/fewer-michiganders-are-enrolling-in-affordable-care-act-health-insurance-plans-this-year · https://www.michiganpublic.org/health/2026-01-05/michiganders-face-higher-health-insurance-premiums-this-new-year

## 12. Behavioral health, CCBHC, and the PIHP system

**Status [S]:** MDHHS's plan to competitively rebid the PIHPs was **upheld by the Court of Claims on Jan. 8, 2026**, but MDHHS **withdrew the RFP** (reported Feb. 12, 2026). The existing PIHP structure remains, and the future approach is unclear (MHA). **The 2026 status of CCBHC expansion is unverified.** MHA supports **HB 6022** (VanderWall, R), which would let Medicaid health plans run pre-admission screening, require CMHSP screening within 3 hours, and otherwise allow hospital staff to screen. Testimony was in House Rules in June 2026 [V]. The FY27 budget includes $31M for behavioral health capacity tied to a new state psychiatric hospital [S].

**Direction [I]:** Benson's MDHHS may revisit PIHP reform, carefully. Boarding and ED-psych holds remain a hospital priority, and CCBHC growth is likely continued if financing survives H.R.1.

## 14. Medical malpractice caps

**Status [S]:** Michigan's noneconomic damages caps remain in place. Bills to raise them, **SB 1158 (Sen. Jeff Irwin, D)** and **HB 6085**, advanced in committee in **Dec. 2024 lame duck** but did not pass. ## 15. MHA's stated 2026 priorities (public)

I found no single published 2026 agenda document. MHA newsroom positions in 2025–26 [V]:
- **Protect Medicaid/HMP funding and recognition of hospital provider taxes** in state budgets, including SNAF, rural and OB stabilization pools, and Maternal Levels of Care.
- **Oppose HBs 6116–6119** (hospital cost review board, M&A tax, price caps). The Board urged members to contact lawmakers.
- **Pass 340B contract pharmacy protections (SB 94)**, which MHA describes as "MHA-driven."
