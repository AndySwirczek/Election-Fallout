# Election Fallout: Leader Brief

**What Michigan's new majority said about health care, Nov. 3–4, 2026.** This covers only what was said election night and the day after, with a plain summary. The full 20-page report (`election_health_2026-11-04.pdf`) has every statement; the 3–4 page version is `election_health_2026-11-04_brief.pdf`.

## Summary of what was said

- **Three of six leaders on camera talked about health care:** Abdul El-Sayed (21 statements), Debbie Dingell (5) and Jocelyn Benson (2). Winnie Brinks, Curtis Hertel Jr. and Mallory McMorrow did not mention it.
- **El-Sayed** said the first thing to do is stop the federal Medicaid cuts scheduled for January 2027, said people who lose Medicaid end up in emergency rooms, and said he expects some rural Republicans to support reversing the cuts. He called for Medicare for All and criticized Blue Cross Blue Shield's market share and rate increases.
- **Dingell** said reversing the Medicaid cuts will be "one of the first things" the new U.S. House majority does, along with reinstating the ACA premium tax credit.
- **Benson** listed health care among costs to make affordable and pledged health care access for women and girls.
- **Puri** said he "watched healthcare be ripped away from our sick" (Michigan Advance).
- **Savit** did not mention health care in his Nov. 4 victory remarks.
- No leader mentioned hospitals, nurses or reimbursement, and no one named a specific state health bill.
