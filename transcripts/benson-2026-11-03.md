# Jocelyn Benson: Election Night and Post-Election Remarks

- **Speaker:** Jocelyn Benson (D), Michigan Secretary of State, Governor-elect
- **Office won:** Governor of Michigan (succeeds term-limited Gov. Gretchen Whitmer; inauguration Jan. 1, 2027 in Lansing per transition site)
- **Event:** Election-night victory speech at the Michigan Democratic Party watch party, Hudson's Detroit (the "Department at Hudson's"), Detroit. Delivered early Wednesday, Nov. 4, 2026; WOOD/Yahoo puts it at about 1:20 a.m. The AP called the race at 1:45 a.m. EST, and Michigan Public says the crowd stayed past 1 a.m. Unofficial results at 1:20 a.m. had Benson at 53.8% and James at 44.5% (Michigan Advance).
- **Completeness:** **Quotes only.** No full transcript was found in text form. The full speech is only on video.
- **Method caveat:** Pages were read through an automated fetch tool that pulls quoted text out of each page. Every quote below appeared in quotation marks in the named source. Some outlets, and the tool itself, cut quotes at about 125 characters, so several quotes are fragments. Check against the video before publishing any quote.

## Post-election statements (Nov. 4)

**Transition announcement** (CBS Detroit, updated 10:33 a.m. Nov. 4; Lansing City Pulse):
- "I'm honored to be Michigan's next governor."
- "Over the course of this campaign, we have heard from people across the state who want to join our team" (Lansing City Pulse)
- "We are looking for hardworking and talented applicants who are committed to making government work well" / "and who share our vision of lowering costs and growing our economy." (Lansing City Pulse; CBS Detroit)

**Transition site** (https://mi2027transition.com/):
- Header: "Now the work begins."
- PARAPHRASE: The guiding priorities are lower costs, higher wages and government that works for residents, with every decision judged by whether it makes life better for Michiganders. The site invites applications for department, governor's office and board or commission posts. The inauguration celebration is Jan. 1 in Lansing.
- The site has **no health-related content.**
- **Transition team:** Only Benson and Brinks are named. No co-chairs or members have been announced in any source found. WLNS (Tim Skubick, Oct. 14) reported she was forming a transition team before the election (https://www.wlns.com/skubick/benson-forming-transition-team-ahead-of-midterms/), but that page was not fetched.
- **MDHHS director:** No announcement and no hints found. For context, Elizabeth Hertel left as MDHHS director June 30, 2026, and Whitmer named **Amy Epkey** (formerly senior deputy director for financial operations) acting director from July 1 (Michigan Public, June 22, 2026: https://www.michiganpublic.org/health/2026-06-22/amy-epkey-to-serve-as-acting-director-of-mdhhs-after-elizabeth-hertel-announces-shes-leaving). ## Campaign platform: healthcare (pre-election, with source)

These are campaign positions, not election-night remarks.

**Medicaid, Healthy Michigan Plan and federal H.R.1 cuts**
- Campaign issues page: says federal officials are "ripping away Medicaid for hundreds of thousands of people"; casts a Democratic governor as a firewall against federal cuts. (https://www.jocelynbenson.com/issues/Healthcare)
- Ad (about Oct. 1, 2026): "Too many families can't afford healthcare, and we need to change that." "Jocelyn Benson will actually put patients first." "And protect Medicaid from cuts hurting seniors and kids." "You work hard for your healthcare. It should work for you." The campaign called health care the "defining issue" of 2026. (https://www.jocelynbenson.com/news/new-ad-jocelyn-benson-vows-to-fight-for-affordable-healthcare-and-put-patients-first)
- Traverse City workforce roundtable (Sept. 21, 2026): says the "Big Beautiful Bill" "was designed to take people off healthcare and make this harder" and that "the federal cuts to our healthcare system are hurting hospitals." The campaign said James "voted for the largest Medicaid cut in history," putting about 500,000 Michiganders at risk (a campaign claim). (https://www.jocelynbenson.com/news/jocelyn-benson-shares-plan-to-tackle-healthcare-workforce-shortage-and-protect-michiganders-from)
- Enrollment (Bridge, July 30, 2026): she would fund "navigators and community health workers at MDHHS to keep people enrolled" and work with neighboring governors to pool resources. (https://bridgemi.com/michigan-politics/where-michigan-governor-candidates-stand-on-health-care-costs-access/)
- PARAPHRASE (WLNS, per search summary; the page returned 403): she would use data the state already collects to keep residents on Medicaid instead of making them reverify repeatedly. She did not say how she would pay to maintain coverage if federal funds fall, saying she would find room in the budget. (https://www.wlns.com/your-local-election-hq/michigan-gubernatorial-race-benson-james/)
- MDHHS: the issues page calls MDHHS "broken," too complicated, outdated and disconnected, and proposes fixing access first using her Secretary of State customer-service model. A related quote: "I'll do the same as governor to make sure every agency is as efficient and customer service oriented as our office is," (Michigan Public, as aggregated on the Feb. 10 campaign release).
- No source used the name "Healthy Michigan Plan."

**Hospital costs, price transparency and surprise bills** (Feb. 10, 2026 plan, as reported by WWMT and quoted on the campaign site)
- PARAPHRASE: Require hospitals to show patients their costs upfront, create an online portal to report hospitals that don't comply, and have the Attorney General enforce. Fight "unfair" medical bills. Support cost-transparency legislation to end surprise bills. (https://www.jocelynbenson.com/news/benson-plan-black-maternal-healthcare-crisis-lower-medical-bills; issues page)
- Bridge: both Benson and James back requiring hospitals and providers to disclose full patient costs at the time of care.
- "This isn't about new spending. It's about priorities – and investing smarter to save lives and lower costs in the long run," (WWMT, via the campaign release)
- **Hospital funding:** No specific hospital funding or rate commitment was found beyond the line that federal cuts are "hurting hospitals."

**Prescription drugs and PBMs**
- An independent Prescription Drug Affordability Board to review pricing and approve price increases (issues page; plan released Jan. 2026 per election.do). Bridge describes it as a board to "hold pharmaceutical companies accountable for price gouging and identify ways to reduce the overall cost of care."
- "As governor, Jocelyn will cut healthcare costs by holding Big Pharma and insurers accountable for price gouging." (Sept. 21 campaign release)
- **PBMs and insulin caps:** Not found in any source fetched.

**Medical debt**
- Expand medical debt relief or forgiveness, meaning government buys residents' medical debt below face value. (Issues page; Bridge)
- No source found tying this to medical-debt work in her Secretary of State tenure.

**Insurers, prior authorization and denials**
- PARAPHRASE: Ban or prohibit insurers from denying or refusing coverage of needed treatment. (Oct. 1 ad release)
- "Prior authorization" by name was **not found.**

**Workforce, nurse staffing and rural care**
- Invest in the healthcare workforce in shortage areas and specialties, expand rural services through mobile health clinics, and fund workforce development (Sept. 21). Offer loan forgiveness and repayment incentives for rural doctors. Partner with providers on mobile health centers bringing low- or no-cost preventive services to "rural and underserved areas" (Bridge).
- The Michigan Nurses Association endorsed her on Feb. 16, 2026. Her quote: "We have a moral duty to serve them just as they show up for us and as governor, I will always have their backs." (Michigan Advance via Yahoo: https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/michigan-nurses-association-endorses-benson-130003830.html)
- **Nurse ratios and mandatory overtime:** No explicit Benson position on the Safe Patient Care Act was found. **Watch item for MHA:** MNA's top priority is that bill, and with a Democratic trifecta it could move.

**Behavioral health**
- Fund wraparound mental-health services in schools for youth, and use mobile clinics "to expand access to quality and affordable care." (Bridge)

**Maternal health, aging and caregivers**
- Have Medicaid cover doula and midwife services; require bias and equity training for providers; invest in culturally competent care (Bridge; Feb. 10 release). "Black women in our state are nearly three times as likely to die from pregnancy-related causes than white women," "When moms get care early, we prevent costly emergencies later." "As leaders in Michigan, we have a moral responsibility to fix this,"
- Revive a $5,000 caregiver tax credit and build a counseling center inside MDHHS for late-life decisions (Bridge).
- Expand low- or no-cost preventive care statewide (issues page).

## Campaign platform: other priorities

- The core message was "costs down, wages up," plus ending corruption: "The heart of our campaign … is costs down, wages up, and fighting for the people of Michigan and ending corruption," "As governor, I will work for you — not the highest bidder, not Donald Trump — you and the people of this state." (Bridge, from the closing bus tour)
- Bridge reports both candidates proposed a new school funding model, tax cuts and a regulatory overhaul.
- Context from Michigan Advance via Yahoo (https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/return-trifecta-michigan-democrats-reclaim-100617155.html): Democrats regained a trifecta. The Senate will make a Michigan Voting Rights Act its first priority. House Democrats name affordability as their top priority and are weighing FOIA reform for the governor's office and Legislature. The likely House Speaker, Ranjeev Puri, said: "I watched healthcare be ripped away from our sick".
