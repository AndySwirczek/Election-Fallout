# Abdul El-Sayed: Election Night Remarks, Post-Election Statements, and Healthcare Platform

- **Speaker:** Abdul El-Sayed (D), physician and epidemiologist. Former Detroit health director and Wayne County health official.
- **Office won:** U.S. Senate, Michigan. This is the open seat of retiring Sen. Gary Peters. He defeated Republican Mike Rogers and will be the first Muslim U.S. senator (AP via PBS, Bridge).
- **Event:** Victory speech at his election-night watch party in Detroit, early Wednesday, Nov. 4, 2026. Per Bridge Michigan, he declared victory about an hour before the AP called the race at 2:27 a.m. Bridge reported a lead of nearly 6 points with 85% counted. Bloomberg, as summarized in search results, had him at 52.5% to 45.6% with about 96% counted. Bloomberg's own page returned 403.
- **Completeness:** **Quotes only.** No text transcript was found. The full speech is only on video, and no YouTube captions were pulled.
- **Method caveat:** Pages were read through an automated fetch tool that extracts quoted text. Every quote below appeared in quotation marks in the named source. Outlets differ slightly on wording, and those differences are noted. Some quotes are fragments. Check against video before publishing.

## 1. Election-night victory speech: verbatim quotes

Wording variants across outlets are noted where they occur.

**Opening and thanks**
- "Michigan, we did it!" (RTE/AFP, Times of Israel). Also reported as "Michigan, we did it" (Bridge, WXYZ, Common Dreams) and "We did it." (Michigan Public live blog)
- "My name is Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed," (Times of Israel)
- "And I am so proud and honored to stand here as your senator-elect for the great state of Michigan." (Times of Israel). Variants: "I am so proud and honoured to stand here as your senator-elect from the great state of Michigan." (RTE/AFP) and "I am so proud and honored to stand here as your senator-elect," (Michigan Public)
- "You took a chance on the unlikeliest of candidates," (AP/PBS)
- "Thank you. This is all yours." (AP/PBS)
- He thanked "everybody who knocked a door, made a call, sent a dollar, posted a story" and those who "gave us your stake in democracy." (Common Dreams)
- "I cannot wait to serve you with the same big-heartedness, open-mindedness, grit and earnestness that you've shown me." (Bridge, WXYZ)
- On saying "inshallah" during the race: "...tonight we can change that Inshallah to 'Alhamdulillah,'" (Times of Israel). Complex renders it as "we can turn that 'inshallah' into a 'alhamdulillah.'" followed by "For the right-wing ops, that just means 'Praise be to God.'"

**On the campaign against him and cost of living**
- Republicans "went all in on one of the ugliest campaigns that we've seen in our lifetimes." (AP/PBS). Fox and Common Dreams have "one of the ugliest campaigns we've seen in our lifetimes."
- "my name and my faith are not why you can't afford your gas." (AP/PBS)
  - Common Dreams: "We all know that my name and my faith aren't the reason you can't afford your gas," then "Not the reason you can't afford your groceries," and "Not the reason you can't see a doctor."
  - Michigan Public: "My name and faith are not the reason you can't afford gas,"
  - Fox: "They might have been pointing at my name and my faith, but we all know my name and my faith aren't the reason"
- "To all the parents who struggled to explain why the president openly mocks someone for their name or how they pray" (Al Jazeera), continuing "let them know tomorrow that the bad guys lost." (Fox)
- "They tried to confuse you, so let me just set the record straight," ... "I love America" (NBC)
- "They want an America that is too small for some of us, but we know America is big enough for all of us," (Fox)
- Michigan Public paraphrases him as saying he loves the country "enough to ask her to be America for all her kids." Only the clause in quotation marks is verbatim.

**Money in politics and the movement**
- "We showed that in a battle between the money and the many, that the many really can win." (Bridge). Al Jazeera has "...that the many can win."
- "We built a movement, government of the people, by the people, and for the people," (Bridge)
- Voters "built something bigger than a bank account." (Bridge)
- **"This movement for money out of politics, money in your pocket, and Medicare for All"** (Common Dreams). This is the only explicit Medicare for All line from the speech found in text.

**Taxes and spending, including the only other healthcare reference**
- **"We want our taxes spent here, for schools here, and healthcare here, and infrastructure here."** (Times of Israel)
- "We don't want our money sent to do apartheid, or genocide, or war," (Times of Israel). Fox has "We don't want our money spent on apartheid or genocidal war,"
- "It shouldn't be this hard to pay your taxes and know that money is going to be spent on you and your kids" (Al Jazeera). Times of Israel adds a contrast with "dropping bombs on somebody else and their kids."

**Remarks to Rogers, Trump and Vance**
- "So Mike, enjoy your retirement," ... "See, you chose hate, and Michigan chose hope. May we never have to see the likes of you in this state again." (NBC). Common Dreams adds: "I know you got a house in Florida and two yachts." Bridge and Fox have "in the state again."
- "There is this delicious irony in the fact that you're constitutionally obligated to swear me in in January." ... "So you know what that means. Wolverine one. Buckeye zero." (Fox, addressing JD Vance)
- "Donald, I know you struggled with my name. So after JD swears me in in January, you can just go on to call me 'Senator.'" (Al Jazeera)

## 4. Healthcare platform (pre-election, sourced)

**Medicare for All: core design**
- Campaign release, Sept. 19, 2026: his plan would "expand Medicare to cover all necessary healthcare," including vision, dental and hearing, for "every single American from cradle to grave" and "without premiums, copays, or deductibles." It also says he "wrote the book on Medicare for All." Source: https://abdulforsenate.com/2026/09/abdul-wrote-the-book-on-how-to-bring-down-healthcare-costs-big-pharmas-champion-mike-rogers-spent-his-career-spiking-them/
- Current Affairs interview, Sept. 28, 2026 (https://www.currentaffairs.org/news/abdul-el-sayed-on-why-medicare-for-all-is-non-negotiable):
  - "Medicare for All is about guaranteed baseline public health coverage with no copays, no deductibles, no premiums"
  - "This is a public guarantee."
  - "It would be available for everyone who pays into the system."
  - It would cover "all medically necessary care and dental care and vision care, mental health care."
  - "it would be accepted by all clinicians." ... "No in-network or out-of-network."
  - "I don't believe in Medicare Advantage."
  - He called Buttigieg-style "Medicare for All Who Want It" "a public option—not a public guarantee."
- **Private insurance shift:** Bridge Michigan (Apr. 29, 2026) reports that earlier in 2026 he said workers could keep supplemental private coverage from unions or employers, which departs from the Sanders bill. CBS 60 Minutes (Nov. 1, 2026) reports, in its own words, that his version "would leave private health insurance options intact" and would be partly funded by "a sweeping tax on billionaire wealth." Source: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/abdul-el-sayed-mike-rogers-michigan-senate-60-minutes-transcript/
  - From the same 60 Minutes transcript: "No. I'm a capitalist who understands the idea that you should have guaranteed healthcare in the country..."
- **Cost and financing:**
  - "Medicare for all would be cheaper than the current system," and "That overhead goes away. No CEOs, no marketing budget, no army of billers — that all goes away." (Michigan Public, Ann Arbor rally, Aug. 27, 2026: https://www.michiganpublic.org/politics-government/2026-08-27/el-sayed-running-for-us-senate-promotes-medicare-for-all-at-ann-arbor-rally)
  - Search summaries cite Fox News and NBC's *Meet the Press* saying he acknowledged some higher taxes for workers. NewsNation's headline says it "shouldn't raise taxes for most families" (page 403). Neither was verified verbatim.
  - No detailed financing plan was found.
- **Bridge, Apr. 29, 2026** (https://bridgemi.com/michigan-government/medicare-for-all-divides-democrats-in-michigans-us-senate-race/):
  - "I believe, as a doctor who understands the system, who's worked in the system, that we can do this."
  - "Your job is not to say what is popular. Your job is to make popular what is right."
  - Context from the same article: expired ACA subsidies meant 34,000 fewer Michigan ACA sign-ups, and KFF projects about 300,000 fewer insured Michiganders from the federal Medicaid changes.
- **Eve-of-election remarks** (AFP via Yahoo, Nov. 3: https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/healthcare-fires-michigan-democrats-eve-025919314.html):
  - "You deserve to be able to see a doctor without getting a huge bill on the back,"
  - "give it up for public health."
  - AFP also reports he pledged to expand Medicare and Medicaid to all Americans.

**H.R. 1 (OBBBA) Medicaid cuts**
- The campaign release (Sept. 19) attacks Rogers for supporting "cuts through the One Big Beautiful Bill that are ripping away healthcare" from 260,000+ Michiganders.
- *Unverified:* a search-engine summary says his campaign site pledges to "immediately repeal Medicaid and ACA subsidy cuts" from OBBBA. I did not see the page text (abdulforsenate.com/issues returned 404), so treat this as unverified.

**Rural hospitals**
- *Paraphrase, secondary source:* a Guardian-derived report (aroundprague.cz) says that while campaigning in the Upper Peninsula he said the cuts are expected to close hospitals in rural places like Escanaba. A separate search summary says that on Oct. 28 in Wyandotte he argued the changes would put more pressure on rural communities. Neither was verified verbatim.
- No specific rural-hospital plan was found.
- Context: MHA's own estimate that OBBBA costs Michigan hospitals more than $6 billion over a decade appeared in coverage, but not attributed to him.

**Hospitals and provider payment**
- None of his own statements on hospital payment rates or the transition were found.
- Bridge notes that public-option and buy-in models "could mean paying hospitals and doctors less." That is Bridge's framing, not his.
- His book argues that a single government plan would let Medicare set rates unilaterally (review summary). This is the most hospital-relevant signal.

**Drug prices**
- No standalone drug-pricing proposal was found. The campaign attacked Rogers as having "voted 19 times to keep prescription drug prices high" and for opposing Medicare negotiation.
- Medicare for All advocates, per Bridge, cite negotiated drug and provider prices as a cost saver.

**Public health record (Detroit / Wayne County)**
- Campaign: "As a public health official, Abdul erased tens of millions of dollars in medical debt," plus a program that "seeks to erase up to $700 million in medical debt."
- Bridge: the Wayne County medical-debt program has cleared over $95 million for more than 100,000 residents since 2024, short of the $700M goal, and he later changed his messaging. He also ran free Narcan distribution and free glasses for Detroit kids.
- He rebuilt the Detroit Health Department after the city's bankruptcy (book author bio).
- He served on the Biden-Sanders Unity Task Force on health care (campaign bio, per search).

**Books**
- **Correction: no book titled "Healthcare for All" was found.** His health-policy book is *Medicare for All: A Citizen's Guide* (Oxford University Press, 2021), co-written with Micah Johnson, MD. Reviews describe:
  - universal coverage as the first of "six ingredients";
  - single-payer rate-setting power as the cost-control mechanism;
  - a grassroots political path to passage.
- He also wrote *Healing Politics* (2020), which is not health-policy specific.
- In 2018, as a candidate for governor, he proposed a state single-payer plan, "MichCare," that would seek federal Medicaid and ACA waivers for financing (Charles Gaba Substack: https://charlesgaba.substack.com/p/flashback-a-deep-dive-into-abdul).
- In 2022 he testified to the Senate Budget Committee on Medicare for All (Ford School).
